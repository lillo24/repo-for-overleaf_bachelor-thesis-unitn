\section{Qualitative Case Analysis of Retrieval Improvements}
\label{sec:qualitative-case-analysis}

Aggregate metrics show whether rewriting improves retrieval overall, but they do not explain why. The following representative cases illustrate recurring mechanisms observed in the judged runs. Exact aggregate values and run-to-run differences are reported only in the consolidated results table; this section focuses on the retrieval behaviour itself.

Across the examples, the rewrite performs two complementary operations. It removes generic conversational structure while preserving the information need, then adds a small number of terms likely to occur in official institutional sources. For each case, the exact cleaned and augmentation layers from one representative frozen rewrite are shown; BM25 receives their concatenation. The LLM is not asked to answer the question.

\subsection{Study-Plan Deadlines}

\textbf{Original query:} \emph{Quali sono le scadenze per la presentazione o la modifica del piano di studi?}\\
\textbf{Cleaned query:} \emph{scadenze presentazione modifica piano di studi}\\
\textbf{Augmentation:} \emph{termini compilazione piano di studio Esse3 periodo inserimento modifiche}

\begin{failurebox}
The baseline was vulnerable to generic bureaucratic expressions such as \emph{quali sono}, \emph{presentazione}, and \emph{scadenze}. These terms occur in many unrelated pages about scholarships, housing, and administrative forms, so lexical overlap did not reliably identify the study-plan workflow.
\end{failurebox}

\begin{effectbox}
The rewrite introduced vocabulary tied to the actual procedure, such as \emph{compilazione}, \emph{piano di studio}, \emph{periodo}, and \emph{Esse3}. This made the study-plan procedure page and related regulation more competitive in the top-three window.
\end{effectbox}

The improvement comes from specialising a generic deadline question toward a concrete administrative process.

\subsection{Finding an Internship Host}

\textbf{Original query:} \emph{Come posso trovare un'azienda o un ente disponibile per un tirocinio?}\\
\textbf{Cleaned query:} \emph{come trovare azienda ente disponibile tirocinio}\\
\textbf{Augmentation:} \emph{ricerca strutture ospitanti elenco aziende convenzioni tirocini}

\begin{failurebox}
The expressions \emph{come posso} and \emph{trovare} matched unrelated FAQ headings. The overlap was lexically strong but did not represent the student's information need.
\end{failurebox}

\begin{effectbox}
The rewrite mapped the student's wording to institutional expressions such as \emph{struttura ospitante}, \emph{soggetto ospitante}, and \emph{convenzione}. These terms occur in internship guidelines and pages describing how host organisations are identified and contacted.
\end{effectbox}

This is a lexical translation between everyday wording and administrative terminology, not only punctuation removal or stemming.

\subsection{Language-Certificate Recognition}

\textbf{Original query:} \emph{Posso usare un certificato per i corsi d'inglese aggiunti a Libretto, come il B2 o C1?}\\
\textbf{Cleaned query:} \emph{posso usare certificato per corsi d inglese aggiunti Libretto B2 C1}\\
\textbf{Augmentation:} \emph{riconoscimento certificazioni linguistiche Libretto esami idoneità livello}

\begin{failurebox}
The baseline matched pages mentioning English levels or course language requirements, but these sources did not address whether an external certificate could be recognised for the student's academic record.
\end{failurebox}

\begin{effectbox}
The augmentation supplied the institutional concept \emph{riconoscimento certificazioni linguistiche}. This aligned the query with the CLA terminology used for certificate recognition and made directly useful sources competitive.
\end{effectbox}

The rewrite contributes a missing administrative concept that a human reader infers from the question but BM25 cannot infer without lexical evidence.

\subsection{Scholarship Requirements}

\textbf{Original query:} \emph{Quali sono i requisiti per accedere alle borse di Opera?}\\
\textbf{Cleaned query:} \emph{quali requisiti accedere borse di Opera}\\
\textbf{Augmentation:} \emph{requisiti richiesti idoneità merito benefici}

\begin{failurebox}
The original query retrieved scholarship-related material, but generic question syntax and broad domain overlap also allowed incomplete FAQs or neighbouring intents to compete. A longer augmentation containing terms about applications, rankings, deadlines, taxes, and calls broadened the intent too far.
\end{failurebox}

\begin{effectbox}
A focused augmentation used terms aligned with eligibility, such as \emph{requisiti richiesti}, \emph{idoneità}, \emph{merito}, and \emph{benefici}. This improved access to the section that directly structures the eligibility requirements.
\end{effectbox}

This case shows that augmentation quality depends on selectivity rather than length.

\subsection{Cross-Case Interpretation}

The cases reveal three recurring mechanisms.

\paragraph{Generic syntax creates cross-domain collisions.} Expressions such as \emph{quali sono}, \emph{come posso}, \emph{trovare}, and \emph{presentazione} occur across many institutional FAQs and can dominate a lexical score, especially in the noisy corpus.

\paragraph{Useful rewrites add administrative concepts, not arbitrary synonyms.} The strongest additions identify concrete workflows or institutional labels, such as study-plan compilation, host organisations, certificate recognition, and scholarship eligibility.

\paragraph{Rewriting does not remove retrieval-side limits.} Missing sources, weak segmentation, duplicate yearly documents, field weighting, and corpus noise can still prevent the intended source from ranking highly. Query rewriting improves the input to BM25; it does not replace a well-designed index.

Overall, these cases support the role of the LLM as a thin semantic layer over deterministic retrieval. Its purpose is to reshape informal student language into a lexical form that better matches official document structure and terminology.
